**Libell 1.5.0** — Libell has moved to its own address, **libell.app**, and starts faster. You choose whether the app may count its use anonymously, you can see and delete everything it has stored, and the Lippert LEVEL9 adjustable ramp is supported. The app now updates itself while it is open.

Open it at **https://libell.app/** — an installed app updates itself, also while it stays open.

## Highlights

- **New address: libell.app.** The app is served from the root of its own domain. The old address, moggleif.github.io/libell, sends you there. A browser keeps saved data per address, so vehicles and settings saved under the old address do not carry over: set them up again, or open a setup link shared from the old app.
- **Anonymous counting is your choice.** On the first start, before anything else, one screen explains what would be counted (that the app is opened, the version, the language, the sensor and which features are used, at most once per start) and what is never sent, and asks **Yes, count anonymously** or **No thanks**. Nothing is sent before you choose, and nothing ever after "No thanks". A browser that sends Global Privacy Control or Do Not Track is never asked and never counted. Change it any time under Help › Privacy.
- **See and delete what Libell has stored.** Help has a new **Privacy** tab: every vehicle, setting, calibration, zero and target the app keeps on the phone, in plain words (the box password masked), a **Show raw data** view, and a red **Delete all Libell data** that asks once and starts the app over as new.
- **A privacy policy in every language,** at libell.app/privacy/, linked from the Privacy tab.
- **Adjustable ramps: Lippert LEVEL9.** The nine-step LEVEL9 is in the ramp list, and its steps wrap so they fit a small phone. For an adjustable ramp, a line above the vehicle says "Set the ramps to the step shown, then drive up", and if the vehicle is still not level once up, tells you to drive off so the app can measure again. The Help's Ramps topic explains it.
- **Updates on its own.** The app now looks for a new version on every start, on return to the foreground, half a minute after that, and every ten minutes while open, and reloads into it straight away.

## Smaller things you may notice

- **A welcome screen on the first visit.** Before the setup guide, one screen says what Libell does, with **Get started**, **Install** and **Look around first**.
- **Install has its own row** under the top bar instead of crowding the warning lamps; its ✕ hides it for good.
- **Faster start.** The first screen loads only what it needs, and only the dictionary of the language in use. Settings, the guide, the "?" page and the sensor box pages load after.
- **Nothing jumps on the first visit,** and the main buttons' text has better contrast.
- **Findable.** Each language has a page at libell.app/<language>/ that explains Libell and answers common questions, and a shared Libell link shows a preview image.

## Under the hood

- Large Clean Code pass: one dialog shell for the overlays, shared builders for buttons, paragraphs and links, one connect/reconnect flow for both sensor boxes, and the kloss fields read in one place — with the behaviour pinned by tests before each change.
- A start-up size budget and Lighthouse checks guard load time and PageSpeed scores.
- Dependabot patch and minor updates merge themselves on green CI (ADR 0023).
- 2726 unit tests, up from 2297 at 1.4.0.

## Honest limitations

- Unchanged: the EasyLevel support is built against the box's decompiled protocol and exercised with a simulator — **no physical EasyLevel box has ever been connected to this codebase.** The Xparkle box has been tested on one real box on Android.
- The Lippert LEVEL9's nine positions are not given by any source we found; the app assumes 2 cm per step. The adjustable-ramp line has not been tried on a real vehicle.
- The automatic update reloads the app at once, also mid-levelling or with a sensor box connected.
- None of the non-Swedish dictionaries has been reviewed by a native speaker yet. Anything that reads wrong is a one-line fix — please report it under **Feedback**.
- A sensor box needs Web Bluetooth: Chrome or Edge on Android or a computer. On iPhone it works through the Bluefy browser.
- The app deliberately refuses to run inside an iframe, so online "mobile viewer" sites show a blank page. That is the clickjacking guard, not a fault.
