**Libell 1.0** — the first stable release. Libell now levels the whole family of vehicles — motorhomes and caravans, single axles and boggies — and its calibration finally understands the difference between a crooked phone and a crooked table.

Open it at **https://moggleif.github.io/libell/** — if you already have it installed, it updates itself on the next start.

## Highlights

- **Caravan mode.** Choose Caravan under **☰ → Settings → Vehicle** and the diagram becomes a caravan: drawbar and jockey wheel up front, one axle pair. Side/side is fixed with a ramp under the low wheel as usual; front/back says **Crank up/Crank down** with the amount in mm — the jockey wheel adjusts both ways, so it is never "red".
- **Boggie (tandem) axles.** New Axle choice: Single or Boggie, for tag-axle motorhomes and tandem caravans alike. The diagram draws the wheel pair, and the recommendation explicitly means _equal steps under both wheels_ — which is also the safe way to ramp a bogie.
- **Calibrate the spot, not just the phone.** The old calibration zeroed the phone — not the table it lies on. New second layer: level the vehicle verifiably once, put the phone in its normal place, tap **Set current position as level**. From then on a slightly tilting table is cancelled out too. The two layers are independent: recalibrating the phone never breaks the vehicle zero.
- **Calibration you can trust over time.** Each calibration shows its age ("14 days ago") and has a **Check** button that answers plainly: "Still good — off by 0.1°" or "Off by 0.8° — consider recalibrating."
- **"Measuring…" instead of flickering advice.** When people walk around inside and the vehicle rocks, the diagram dims and the status says Measuring… until the reading has been calm for a moment. No more advice that changes with every footstep — and no celebration triggered by a wobble.
- **Clearer wheel guidance.** Above each wheel: which step to drive up onto — **Step 2** — with the height in small parentheses; below: the lift in bold mm. The first-run introduction now also teaches the color language (green ✓ / orange ↑ / red ✕) and the bubble.
- **Bigger outdoors.** The diagram and status text scale up to use the screen — readable at arm's length in daylight.
- **Where do I find the measurements?** The settings form now says it: registration document, handbook, or a tape measure — a few cm of error hardly matters. With a boggie, measure to the centre of the axle pair.

## Under the hood

- The leveling math is untouched where it was already right: a boggie is modeled as one axle at its midpoint, the caravan's axle is its reference plane, and the same hysteresis core keeps every displayed value calm. Each decision is recorded in `docs/adr/` (ten records).
- Quality gates on every change: 103 unit tests (from 55), a Playwright smoke test against the built app in CI, CodeQL, pinned actions, strict CSP plus a framing guard against clickjacking.
- Requirements R1–R26 describe every behavior with acceptance criteria, and the docs now have a single source for every fact.

## Getting started

1. Open the app and (on iPhone) tap **Start** to grant motion access — the introduction takes it from there.
2. Pick vehicle, axle and ramp in **☰ → Settings**, check the measurements.
3. Calibrate the phone once, and — first time the vehicle stands verifiably level — set its normal spot as the vehicle zero.
4. Lay the phone flat, top edge toward the front, and follow the wheels.
