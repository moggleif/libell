**Libell 1.2** — it fits on the screen now. 1.1 added a great deal; this release makes it usable on the phone you actually hold. It started with one photograph from an iPhone, where the introduction's **Next** button sat below the fold with no way to reach it, and ended with every screen in the app measured against real phone sizes.

Open it at **https://moggleif.github.io/libell/** — if you already have it installed, it updates itself on the next start.

## Highlights

- **The introduction fits one screen again.** On an iPhone SE in Safari, five of the ten steps were taller than the screen, and because the whole thing scrolled as one column the **Back / Skip / Next** buttons went off the bottom with the content: you reached a step and there was no visible way forward. The step count, the ✕, the heading and the buttons are now pinned and never scroll away, and the steps themselves were trimmed until they fit whole — every word, field, control and illustration still there, in both appearances and all five languages.
- **The level screen fits too.** The vehicle diagram was sized only by its width, so on every phone it came out about as tall as the whole screen and pushed the tilt readout and the action bar below the fold — the diagram was cut off mid-wheel. It is now the elastic part of the screen: it takes the room the rest leaves and no more, while keeping the drawing's exact proportions so each wheel card still sits on the wheel it describes.
- **Nothing hides behind Safari's toolbar any more.** Every full-screen view is now sized to the height you can actually see, with room left for the home indicator. Previously they were sized to the taller "no toolbars" height iOS reports, which put the last button of a page behind the browser's own bar with nothing to scroll.
- **Settings no longer slides sideways.** On a 320-pixel phone the settings page could be dragged left and right, because the row of tabs was wider than the screen and took the page with it. The tabs now swipe within their own strip, and rows that no longer fit — a long German label beside its dropdown, the Reset/Undo/Save row — wrap instead of pushing the page around.
- **The Ramps tab says the chosen ramp once.** It used to name it three times over — a card above the list, its own row inside the list, and the Save block at the bottom — with the step heights spelled out twice. The tab now opens with the answer: which ramp is set and what its steps are, then how many you own. The catalogue itself moved into one disclosure, **Change ramp**, that carries the brand filter and the models it narrows together, so the tab shows what you have rather than asking you to choose again every time you open it. **Custom set** leads that list instead of sitting apart below it, and no brand filter can hide it. A bug found while measuring the tab: on a 393×745 phone the catalogue was **zero pixels tall** — the ramp picker, on the ramp tab, invisible.
- **The stray half-circle is gone.** A small grey half-disc appeared near the top-left corner while a sensor was still starting up. It was the level bubble: it had no starting position of its own, so it was drawn at the very corner of the diagram with half of it cut off, until the first reading moved it. It now starts where it belongs, in the middle of its dial.

## Smaller things you may notice

- **The level screen stopped narrating itself.** "2 wheels to raise", "measuring", "level" — the wheel cards and the bubble already say all of that, in colour, on the same screen. The status line now speaks only when no icon can: when you are within a hair of level, and when your ramps cannot reach.
- **The version moved under the logo** in the top-left corner, in small type, and the footer row it used to occupy is gone.
- **Install** now sits directly beside the sensor icon in the top-right corner, instead of moving down a row whenever a warning lamp lights.
- On the External sensor page, the found sensor is set apart from the **Connect** button instead of sitting tight underneath it.
- That same row no longer pretends to lead somewhere: while the phone's own sensor is in use it is plain text, not a link into a page about a Bluetooth box you have not connected.
- The introduction's "what skipping costs" note now sits next to the **Skip** button it explains, at a size that leaves room for the step itself.
- On a calibration step the heading is no longer printed twice.

## Under the hood

- Nothing about the leveling math, the sensors or your stored settings changed in this release. It is layout, and the checks that keep layout honest.
- A new check (`npm run fit`) opens **every** view — level screen, all ten introduction steps, settings, help, the external sensor page, the iOS guide, a shared setup link — against three phone sizes, both appearances and all five languages, and fails the build on anything below the fold, any page that scrolls sideways, a diagram taller than its space, or a wheel card that has drifted off its wheel. 341 checks per run, on every branch.
- It has a second half that reads the stylesheet directly, because the browser available in CI cannot reproduce the iOS behaviour that caused half of these bugs: there, the visible height and the "no toolbars" height are the same number, so a screen that strands its last button behind Safari's bar looks perfectly fine. That rule is therefore enforced against the source instead.
- 651 unit tests, up from 630 at 1.1.

## Honest limitations

- Unchanged from 1.1: the EasyLevel support is built against the box's decompiled protocol and exercised with a simulator — **no physical box has ever been connected to this codebase.**
- The four non-Swedish dictionaries still have not been reviewed by a native speaker. Anything that reads wrong is a one-line fix — please report it under **Feedback**.
- The Ramps tab is the one screen that still scrolls on the smallest phones: on a 375×553 iPhone SE it runs about 105 px past the bottom. Everything on it is reachable; it is simply a long tab. Its own controls never leave the screen.
- A 320×480 screen (an iPhone 5 or the first SE) is covered for reachability and sideways scrolling, but the longest German calibration step still scrolls its own content there. Making it fit would mean type too small to read. Its buttons stay put either way.
- Libell owns one ramp model at a time: a set of step heights and how many identical ramps you have. If you own two different models, enter the one you want the app to plan with — mixing is not expressed yet.
- The app deliberately refuses to run inside an iframe, so online "mobile viewer" sites show a blank page. That is the clickjacking guard, not a fault — use your browser's own device mode, or a real phone.
