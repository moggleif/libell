**Libell 0.2** is a big usability release: the app now speaks Swedish and English, guides you through the first start, knows the ramps you can actually buy, and stays calm when your motorhome sits right on the level boundary.

Open it at **https://moggleif.github.io/libell/** — if you already have it installed, it updates itself on the next start.

## Highlights

- **Swedish and English.** The whole app follows your phone's language — Swedish on Swedish phones, English otherwise.
- **First-run introduction.** Three short steps — how to place the phone, your vehicle measurements, calibration. Every step can be skipped or the whole thing closed with ✕, and it can be reopened any time from the menu.
- **Ready-made ramp picker.** Choose your leveling ramp from a list of models sold by Swedish camping shops — Thule Levelers (the default), Fiamma Level Up/Premium, Milenco Triple/Trident/Quattro, Froli, Biltema — and the step heights fill themselves in. Fine-tune with the +/− chips if yours differ.
- **Rock-steady display.** Wheel colors, mm figures and the level status change only when the reading has clearly and durably moved — no more flickering wheels, strobing screen or repeated vibrations when parked right on the edge of level. The celebration fires once per actual leveling.
- **Flip calibration.** No level surface around? Capture, rotate the phone 180° on the same spot, capture again — the surface tilt cancels itself out and only the phone's own bias is stored.
- **Pose guard.** If the phone is picked up or turned to landscape the app says so instead of showing meaningless wheel advice — and the menu (☰) and version number stay reachable the whole time.
- **Themes.** Follow the phone, or force light/dark in Settings — with instant preview.
- **Share button** next to the app name: opens the phone's share sheet so you can pass Libell on in two taps.
- **Safer settings.** Undo changes returns to the last saved values; Reset to defaults fills the factory values (nothing is stored until you press Save). Full-screen settings/help pages with a back button that also answers the phone's back gesture.
- **Illustrated help.** Drawings for phone placement, the wheel symbols (✓/↑/✕), the vehicle measurements and the 180° flip — reading glasses optional.
- **Feedback from the menu**: fill in a short form and it opens a ready-made report on GitHub.

## Under the hood

- Display stabilization got a proper hysteresis design: a dead band (the Stability setting) plus dwell times, with the level status derived from the wheel colors so the screen can never contradict itself. Covered by regression tests from real field reports.
- Security hardening: strict Content-Security-Policy, no third-party requests, CodeQL scanning on every change, all CI actions pinned by commit hash, Dependabot watching dependencies.
- All dependencies on current majors (Vite 8, Vitest 4, vite-plugin-pwa 1.3). 55 unit tests.
- The install prompt now shows a real screenshot of the app, generated in CI on every deploy.

## Getting started

1. Open the app and (on iPhone) tap **Start** to grant motion access — the introduction takes it from there.
2. Pick your ramp in **☰ → Settings**, check the measurements, calibrate once on a known-level surface.
3. Lay the phone flat in the vehicle, top edge toward the front, and follow the wheels.
