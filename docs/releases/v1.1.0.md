**Libell 1.1** — the tilt no longer has to come from the phone, the app no longer has to be in Swedish or English, and your ramps no longer have to be typed in millimetre by millimetre. This is the largest release since 1.0: 60 merges, and the screens have been rebuilt around the one thing you actually do out there — level the vehicle and get on with the evening.

Open it at **https://moggleif.github.io/libell/** — if you already have it installed, it updates itself on the next start.

## Highlights

- **An external sensor instead of the phone.** Connect a permanently mounted EasyLevel BLE box under **External sensor** and it reads the tilt for you — no more laying the phone flat and leaving it there every time you park. It brings its own installation calibration ("the vehicle stands level now, so this reading is zero"), a mounting-orientation choice covering all four quarter turns, live battery and temperature, silent reconnection when you reopen the app, and background retries when the link drops. Libell never switches source behind your back: losing the box gives you an explicit **Retry / Use phone sensor** choice, because the two have different calibrations. Requires Chrome on Android with Bluetooth on.
- **Five languages.** Swedish, English, French, Spanish and German. Your phone's language decides automatically, and **Settings → General → Language** overrides it — each language named in itself (Deutsch, English, Español, Français, Svenska), so you can find yours whatever the app currently shows.
- **Pick your ramps from a list.** Eleven ready-made models from the shops people actually buy from — Thule, Fiamma, Milenco, Froli, Biltema — each with its real step heights. Choose yours and the step list fills itself; the chips still let you fine-tune, and a custom set works exactly as before.
- **Rebuilt screens.** The hamburger menu is gone in Modern: a bottom action bar puts settings, sound and help within thumb reach, the gear opens tabbed settings directly, and **?** has its own Help/About/Feedback tabs. Fewer labels, bigger targets, less to read while standing in a field in the dark.
- **A guided start you can correct.** The introduction opens with a welcome and a General step — language, theme, sound — before asking anything about the vehicle. It gained a **Back** button, real calibration UI instead of a description of it, caravan imagery when you pick a caravan, and it now says plainly what skipping costs: a warning lamp stays lit, and the shipped defaults apply meanwhile.
- **Share your setup with the family.** **Settings → Share vehicle setup** makes a link carrying only the measurements and ramps, so a partner using the same motorhome never has to re-measure and retype anything. Calibration is deliberately never included — that belongs to each individual phone.
- **iPhone: a workaround instead of a dead end.** Safari has no Web Bluetooth and Apple has no plans to add it. Rather than hiding the external-sensor feature there, iOS now shows a step-by-step guide to Bluefy, a third-party browser that supplies it; Libell then works unchanged inside it.
- **Numbers you can trust.** If readings stop arriving, the guidance pauses and says so rather than leaving a stale number on screen looking live. And the response delay now adapts — quick while you are actively driving up a ramp, calm when the vehicle is at rest — so the reading keeps up without flickering when someone walks past inside.
- **A third look.** Appearance now offers **Glossy** alongside Classic and Modern, and Classic was restyled as honest plain HTML instead of a half-finished Modern.

## Smaller things you may notice

- The waste-water drain can now be a single **corner** (front left, rear right …), not just a whole side — matching where the outlet actually sits.
- "Your ramps are not enough here" now says _how far_ off you are, so you know whether to nudge the vehicle or find a new pitch.
- A gray wheel says **No ramp** instead of leaving the line blank — it is a low wheel your ramps cannot reach, not a glitch.
- The menu shows a one-line summary of the three offsets level is calculated from, so nothing is hidden arithmetic.
- Saving settings returns you to the level screen, so the effect is visible without an extra tap.
- The level chime now defaults to on.

## Honest limitations

- The EasyLevel support is built against the box's decompiled protocol and exercised with a simulator (`?easylevel-sim`) — **no physical box has ever been connected to this codebase.** If yours misbehaves, the sensor page's Debug info exists for exactly that, including an experimental connect-delay workaround.
- The four non-Swedish dictionaries are translations that no native speaker has reviewed. Anything that reads wrong is a one-line fix — please report it under **Feedback**.

## Under the hood

- The leveling math is unchanged. What moved is where the tilt comes from: a sensor-source seam now sits between the reading and the math, and calibration split three ways — phone sensor, vehicle zero, and the external box's own installation offset — so switching source never silently invalidates a calibration you still rely on.
- 630 unit tests, up from 103 at 1.0, plus a Playwright smoke test against the built app, CodeQL, pinned actions and a strict CSP.
- Requirements R1–R44 describe every behavior with acceptance criteria; 15 ADRs record why. Both are updated in the same change as the code they describe.

## Getting started

1. Open the app and (on iPhone) tap **Start** to grant motion access — the introduction takes it from there.
2. Pick your language, vehicle, axle and ramp model; check the measurements.
3. Calibrate the phone once, and — the first time the vehicle stands verifiably level — set its normal spot as the vehicle zero.
4. Lay the phone flat, top edge toward the front, and follow the wheels.
