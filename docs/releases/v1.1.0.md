**Libell 1.1** — Libell now speaks five languages, can read its tilt from a permanently mounted sensor box instead of the phone, and has had its screens rebuilt around the one thing you actually do with it: level the vehicle and get on with the evening.

Open it at **https://moggleif.github.io/libell/** — if you already have it installed, it updates itself on the next start.

## Highlights

- **Five languages.** Swedish, English, French, Spanish and German. Your phone's language decides automatically, and **Settings → General → Language** overrides it — each language named in itself (Deutsch, English, Español, Français, Svenska) so you can find yours whatever the app currently shows.
- **External sensor support (EasyLevel BLE box).** Instead of laying the phone flat every time, connect a permanently mounted EasyLevel box under **External sensor** and it reads the tilt for you — the phone can stay in your pocket. Includes its own installation calibration ("set the vehicle level once, this reading is now zero"), a mounting-orientation choice covering all four quarter turns, live battery and temperature, and silent reconnection when you reopen the app. Requires Chrome on Android with Bluetooth on.
- **iPhone: a workaround instead of a dead end.** Safari has no Web Bluetooth and Apple has no plans to add it. Rather than hiding the feature, iOS now shows a step-by-step guide to Bluefy, a third-party browser that supplies it — Libell then works unchanged inside it.
- **Share your vehicle setup with the family.** **Settings → Share vehicle setup** produces a link carrying only the measurements and ramps, so a partner using the same motorhome does not have to re-measure and retype anything. Calibration is deliberately never included: that belongs to each phone.
- **Rebuilt screens.** The hamburger menu is gone in Modern: a bottom action bar puts settings, sound and help within thumb reach, the gear opens tabbed settings directly, and "?" has its own Help/About/Feedback tabs. Fewer labels, bigger targets, less to read while standing in a field.
- **A third look.** Appearance now offers **Glossy** alongside Classic and Modern, and Classic was restyled as honest plain HTML rather than a half-finished Modern.
- **A guided start that can be corrected.** The introduction opens with a welcome and a General step (language, theme, sound) before asking anything about the vehicle, gained a **Back** button, and now says plainly what skipping a step actually costs — a warning lamp stays lit, and the shipped defaults are used meanwhile.
- **Stale data is no longer shown as live.** If sensor readings stop arriving, the guidance pauses and says so instead of leaving an old number on screen looking current.
- **Steadier numbers while you drive up.** The response delay now adapts: quick while you are actively adjusting, calm when the vehicle is at rest — so the reading keeps up with the ramp without flickering when someone walks past.

## Honest limitations

- The EasyLevel support is built against the box's decompiled protocol and exercised with a simulator (`?easylevel-sim`), **not against physical hardware** — no real box has been connected to this codebase yet. If yours misbehaves, the sensor page's Debug info exists precisely for that, including an experimental connect-delay workaround.
- The four non-Swedish dictionaries are translations, not native-speaker reviewed. Anything that reads wrong is a one-line fix — please report it through **☰ → Feedback**.

## Under the hood

- The leveling math is unchanged. What moved is where the tilt comes from: a sensor-source seam now sits between the reading and the math, and calibration split three ways (phone sensor, vehicle zero, and the external box's own installation offset) so switching source never silently invalidates a calibration.
- 630 unit tests (from 103 at 1.0), plus a Playwright smoke test against the built app, CodeQL, pinned actions and a strict CSP.
- Requirements R1–R44 describe every behavior with acceptance criteria; 15 ADRs record why. Both are updated in the same change as the code they describe.

## Getting started

1. Open the app and (on iPhone) tap **Start** to grant motion access — the introduction takes it from there.
2. Pick your language, vehicle, axle and ramp; check the measurements.
3. Calibrate the phone once, and — the first time the vehicle stands verifiably level — set its normal spot as the vehicle zero.
4. Lay the phone flat, top edge toward the front, and follow the wheels.
